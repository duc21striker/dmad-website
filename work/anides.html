01 — The Brief

Beautiful product. No brand to hold it together.

Anides Yarn Studios sells handmade crochet pieces — bags, accessories, and made-to-order items. The product itself was warm, tactile, and personal. But there was no brand system binding the business together.

Every order shipped in generic packaging. No consistent logo. No recognisable identity. When customers received an order, there was nothing to remember about the studio beyond the item itself.

We built a brand that matched the warmth of the craft — soft, considered, and instantly recognisable. Now every order that leaves the studio carries a piece of the brand with it.

02 — What We Delivered

Six brand touchpoints.

Not a logo file. A full brand system that works across every piece of physical and digital material the studio produces.

Logo & Wordmark

Hand-drawn feel that matches the tactile nature of the craft — not a sterile geometric mark.

Packaging Labels

Paper bag labels, box inserts, and finishing touches that make every order feel intentional.

Sticker Design

Brand stickers for sealing orders and shared freely by customers — organic marketing.

Colour System

A warm purple-and-pink palette that reads as handmade without being twee.

Typography Pairing

A serif display for warmth and a clean sans for utility — consistent across every touchpoint.

Brand Direction

Photography guidance, tone of voice, and visual references so the brand stays consistent as it grows.

04 — The Approach

Built from the craft outward.

  1. 01

    Study the material

    Before opening a design file, we spent time with the studio's actual pieces — the textures, the yarn colours, the way the crochet felt in hand. Every design decision echoed something from the physical product.

  2. 02

    Design for the unboxing

    For handmade businesses, the packaging is the first time the customer holds the brand. We designed the label, the sticker, and the paper bag together — so the unboxing itself became part of the experience.

  3. 03

    Build a system, not a logo

    The final deliverable wasn't a single logo file. It was a full set of brand assets — colours, fonts, labels, stickers, and photography direction — so the studio could grow without losing consistency.
