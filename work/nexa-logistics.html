01 — The Brief

Corporate clients can't vet a logistics company they can't find.

Nigerian logistics companies compete for corporate contracts — but most websites are one-page brochures that answer none of the questions procurement teams actually ask.

NEXA LOGISTICS demonstrates the level of trust signal that B2B logistics companies need: service breadth, coverage areas, tracking capability, and a direct quote request flow. It's the difference between "we could try them" and "they look serious."

02 — What We Built

Six ways the site closes corporate deals.

Every section answers a question a procurement officer would ask.

Service Breakdown

Same-day, interstate, e-commerce, corporate — separate pages, clear specs.

Coverage & SLA

Per-state delivery times and capabilities. Clarity over vagueness.

Shipment Tracking

A simple tracking UI. Customer enters their waybill number and sees status.

Quote Request Flow

Structured form with fields procurement teams actually need — volumes, routes, frequency.

For Businesses

A dedicated section for corporate accounts — SLAs, integration options, account managers.

Case Studies

Anonymised proof from logistics clients. Volume, sectors, savings.

04 — The Approach

Built for buyers who read everything.

  1. 01

    Start with procurement questions

    Corporate logistics buyers ask the same twelve questions. We mapped them all before designing — then built each section to answer one clearly, without marketing language.

  2. 02

    Show functional depth

    Tracking UI. Quote flow. Coverage map. These aren't decoration — they're proof that the company operates at enterprise scale.

  3. 03

    Design for mobile, build for desktop

    Procurement teams often research on mobile and sign off on desktop. We designed both surfaces with equal weight — no compromises on either.
