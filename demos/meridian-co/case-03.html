Case Study · 03 2024

Expanding a retail group into three new states — without chaos.

How a Port Harcourt–based retail group launched three new locations on schedule and within budget — through disciplined market feasibility, a documented operational playbook, and structured staff training.

At a Glance

A regional retailer with national ambition.

The business had built a strong, profitable chain of 12 stores across Rivers and Bayelsa. The next step — expansion into three new states — had been discussed for two years. Nobody could agree on where, in what order, or how to run a new store without the founder being physically present.

The leadership team came to us with a clear ask: "Help us expand without breaking what already works."

01

The Challenge

Ambitious expansion plans, no market-level evidence, and an operating model that depended entirely on the founder's personal presence.

The current model worked because the founder knew every store, every manager, and every customer pattern. That model could not scale to three new states, 200+ kilometres from headquarters.

Before deciding where to expand — or whether to expand at all — the business needed to solve four foundational issues:

  • 01

    No market-level feasibility data

    Expansion targets had been chosen based on intuition and personal familiarity. There was no structured analysis of demographics, competition, footfall, or purchasing power in any of the proposed markets.

  • 02

    Operations lived in the founder's head

    Store opening procedures, daily operations, stock management, and staffing norms were undocumented. Every existing store had been opened by the founder personally.

  • 03

    No training system for new staff

    New hires learned by shadowing existing staff. There was no standard onboarding, no role-based training, and no formal sign-off on competency before a staff member handled customers alone.

  • 04

    Unclear expansion economics

    The business knew what a store earned, but not what it cost to open one — fit-out, inventory, staffing, marketing, and the runway until breakeven. Capital allocation decisions were being made without a model.

“

We'd been talking about expansion for two years. MERIDIAN & CO. got us from conversation to three open stores in nine months — and the new stores run better than some of our old ones.

Managing Director Retail Group · Port Harcourt
02

Our Approach

We sequenced the work in the right order: evidence first, systems second, people third, launch last. Each phase produced something the business could use.

  1. Phase 01 · Months 1–2

    Market feasibility

    We evaluated six candidate markets against a structured framework — demographics, competition, infrastructure, and cost of entry. Three were selected for expansion and ranked by priority.

    • Six-market feasibility analysis
    • Competitive mapping and pricing study
    • Footfall and demographic modelling
    • Cost-of-entry model per market
    • Ranked expansion recommendation
  2. Phase 02 · Months 3–5

    Operational playbook

    We documented what had been living in the founder's head into a repeatable playbook — from site selection to store opening to daily operations.

    • Site selection and lease evaluation criteria
    • Store fit-out and launch checklist
    • Daily operations manual
    • Stock management and reorder system
    • Store manager reporting rhythm
  3. Phase 03 · Months 6–9

    Training & staged launch

    We built a role-based training programme, trained the first cohort of new store managers, and supported the launch of each new location in sequence — one every six weeks.

    • Role-based training curriculum
    • Store manager certification programme
    • Launch support for 3 new locations
    • Weekly performance review during launch period
    • Post-launch optimisation (3 months)

The Outcome

Three stores. Nine months. On schedule.

Every location launched on time, within the cost-of-entry model, and reached operational breakeven within the projected window.

The business no longer depends on the founder being physically present for a store to open or operate. The playbook runs. The training works. New store managers are certified before they touch a customer.

Twelve months after the engagement ended, the group has opened a fourth location — without external advisory support — and is evaluating a fifth for the following year.
