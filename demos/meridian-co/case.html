Case Study · 01 2024

Turning a declining manufacturer back to profitable growth.

How a mid-sized Nigerian manufacturing group recovered 23% of margin in six months — without cutting headcount, without new capital, and without disrupting production.

At a Glance

A profitable company with an invisible problem.

On paper, the business was healthy. Revenue had grown year over year. Production lines ran at capacity. But margins had quietly eroded for three consecutive years — and nobody inside the business could say exactly why.

Leadership came to us not with a specific ask, but with a question: "Where is our money going?"

01

The Challenge

Three years of declining margins, no clarity on the cause, and a leadership team that had run out of internal answers.

The business operated across four production lines and two distribution centres. Its cost structure had never been reviewed end-to-end since it scaled from a single-line operation six years earlier. Each department tracked its own metrics — but nobody tracked the seams between them.

What we found in the first two weeks of discovery wasn't one problem. It was five, compounding quietly:

  • 01

    Unchecked supplier pricing drift

    Three of the top five raw material suppliers had raised prices twice in 18 months. Neither increase had been benchmarked or renegotiated.

  • 02

    Overproduction on low-margin SKUs

    The highest-volume product line was also the lowest-margin. Production planning had never been aligned to margin — only to units.

  • 03

    Downtime hidden in maintenance logs

    Unplanned downtime accounted for 11% of production hours — but nobody had calculated the cost of that downtime in naira.

  • 04

    Working capital tied up in slow-moving inventory

    ₦340M sat in warehouse stock — 40% of it classified as slow-moving. Nobody had made the decision to clear it.

  • 05

    No single owner of margin

    Sales owned revenue. Production owned output. Finance owned cost. Nobody owned margin — so nobody was accountable for it.

“

We'd been running the business on instinct for years. What MERIDIAN & CO. gave us wasn't a report — it was clarity. For the first time, we could see exactly where the money was going and make deliberate decisions about it.

Managing Director Manufacturing Group · Lagos
02

Our Approach

We ran a twelve-week operational and financial audit, then translated the findings into a cost restructuring programme the leadership team could actually execute.

  1. Phase 01 · Weeks 1–4

    Full operational audit

    We embedded with the operations and finance teams, mapped every cost centre, and traced naira from raw material purchase to finished goods delivery. Every assumption the leadership team held was tested against data.

    • End-to-end cost map across four production lines
    • Supplier pricing benchmark against market rates
    • Margin analysis by SKU and by customer
    • Downtime cost model (naira-per-hour)
    • Working capital review — inventory, receivables, payables
  2. Phase 02 · Weeks 5–8

    Cost restructuring programme

    With clarity on where margin was leaking, we designed a restructuring plan prioritised by impact and speed of implementation. No layoffs. No production disruption.

    • Supplier renegotiation strategy for top 5 vendors
    • Revised production plan aligned to margin, not volume
    • Preventive maintenance programme to reduce downtime
    • Inventory clearance plan for slow-moving stock
    • New margin accountability structure — one owner per line
  3. Phase 03 · Weeks 9–12

    Implementation & measurement

    We didn't hand over a deck and leave. We stayed through implementation, worked alongside department heads, and established the reporting rhythm that would let the business track margin weekly — not quarterly.

    • Weekly margin dashboard (finance + operations)
    • Supplier negotiation support (attended first sessions)
    • Production planning training for line managers
    • Monthly business review template

The Outcome

Six months later.

Not projections. Not modelled estimates. Measured results from the business's own reporting systems.

The business didn't just recover its margin. It built the internal capability to hold it. Weekly margin reporting is now part of the operating rhythm. Suppliers are reviewed on a quarterly cycle. Production planning is aligned to margin — not to units.

Twelve months after the engagement ended, the group reported sustained margin above 30% and has since funded a capacity expansion from internal cash flow — without new debt.
