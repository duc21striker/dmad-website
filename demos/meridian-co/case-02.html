Case Study · 02 2024

Preparing a fintech to raise its first institutional round.

How a Nigerian fintech startup closed a ₦150M seed round in a difficult fundraising market — through investor-ready financials, a disciplined narrative, and a structured approach to outreach.

At a Glance

A strong product with weak financials.

The product worked. Early customers liked it. Revenue was growing. But when the founders started conversations with institutional investors, they kept hearing the same thing: the numbers weren't ready.

Three months of investor outreach had produced meetings, but no term sheet. The founders came to us with a specific question: "What are we missing?"

01

The Challenge

A working product, real customers, and revenue growth — but no investor-ready financial foundation underneath any of it.

The business had grown organically for two years. Decisions had been made on instinct and speed, which is exactly what early-stage companies should do. But the financial infrastructure hadn't kept pace with the product.

When we reviewed their investor materials, we found four structural gaps that were undermining otherwise strong conversations:

  • 01

    No three-statement financial model

    Investors were being shown a revenue projection and a P&L. There was no integrated P&L, balance sheet, or cash flow — and no driver-based logic behind the assumptions.

  • 02

    Unit economics not proven

    The business knew its revenue. It did not know its contribution margin per customer, CAC payback period, or cohort retention by channel. Investors asked; the team couldn't answer.

  • 03

    Narrative didn't match the numbers

    The pitch positioned the company as a fast-scaling platform. The financials told a smaller, slower story. The two weren't reconciled — and investors noticed.

  • 04

    No structured outreach process

    Investor conversations were happening ad hoc, without a pipeline, without tracking, and without a clear sequence from first meeting to term sheet.

02

Our Approach

We rebuilt the financial foundation from the ground up, aligned the narrative to the numbers, and ran a disciplined fundraising process alongside the founders.

  1. Phase 01 · Weeks 1–4

    Financial foundation

    We built a driver-based, three-statement financial model from historicals and rebuilt the unit economics from raw customer data. Every assumption was documented and testable.

    • Three-statement financial model (historical + 3-year projection)
    • Unit economics — CAC, LTV, contribution margin, payback
    • Cohort retention analysis by acquisition channel
    • Scenario modelling — base, upside, downside
    • Data room structure and financial documentation
  2. Phase 02 · Weeks 5–8

    Investor narrative & materials

    With the numbers solid, we rebuilt the pitch. The narrative was rewritten to lead with proven traction and honest growth assumptions — the kind institutional investors actually trust.

    • Rewritten pitch deck (12 slides)
    • One-page executive summary for warm intros
    • Financial narrative memo — 2 pages explaining key metrics
    • Investor FAQ document addressing common objections
    • Rehearsal sessions with the founding team
  3. Phase 03 · Weeks 9–16

    Structured fundraising process

    We built an investor pipeline, prioritised by fit and thesis alignment, and ran the process with the founders — including supporting diligence and term sheet negotiation.

    • Prioritised target investor list (40 firms, tiered)
    • Warm intro strategy and outreach sequencing
    • Diligence response management
    • Term sheet review and negotiation support
    • Post-close investor relations rhythm

The Outcome

Four months, start to close.

From stalled conversations to a signed term sheet and a fully subscribed round — with the financial infrastructure to support the next raise.
